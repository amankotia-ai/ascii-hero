Freebyrd: a Mac assistant that lives in the notch, built around one idea, that nothing you promise should slip.

Personal software | Product design, engineering and film | 2026

Product design and engineering of the Mac app, a Rust app with its notch island in plain HTML; the local memory and the connector that shares it with Claude Code; the explainer film, every frame of it rendered in code

Freebyrd is a Mac assistant that lives in the notch. It reads the window in front of you, keeps a memory of your work on your Mac, read from where it already lives: mail, chats, meetings and Claude Code sessions. Then it follows through on what you promised. I built it for my own days, spent across a dozen apps with several Claude Code sessions running at once.

Work moves between surfaces faster than any list can follow. A promise is made in Mail, its detail sits in Notion, the work runs in a Claude Code session, and the reference that helps was saved on Tuesday. An assistant that waits to be asked arrives too late, and one that interrupts gets turned off. Freebyrd had to know enough to help at the right moment, stay quiet the rest of the time, and keep all of it on the Mac.

I put it where the Mac already has a place nobody uses: the notch. Double-tap Option and the island opens over whatever is in front of you, already holding the window and your work, so you can ask about either. When something needs you, the notch glows, and every card says where it came from. A promise made in a reply lands on the calendar with Undo; a draft is ready before the due date, and Copy is the only way out. Nothing is sent and nothing runs without a press.

The memory stays in one file on the Mac, with secrets taken out before anything is kept, and Claude sees only what a moment needs, through one locked-down door. The same memory opens every Claude Code session where the last one stopped.

The film explains it the way the product feels, one surface at a time, with the island at its true size on a 14 inch MacBook Pro and every container measured from the app itself. Its second half shows where Freebyrd is heading, still concepts today: a picture of how the work moves between apps, things prepared before you reach for them, and a night shift that offers one thing and the morning it buys.
